Sleep

Sleep is essential for teenagers as it supports brain development, improves memory and concentration, regulates emotions and promotes physical growth. Getting sufficient sleep also helps teenagers perform better academically and maintain good mental and physical health. According to sleep experts, teenagers aged 13 to 18 should get 8 to 10 hours of sleep every night.

  1. Get 8 to 10 hours of sleep every night.
  2. Limit caffeine consumption, especially in the evening.
  3. Avoid using electronic devices close to bedtime.
  4. Maintain regular sleeping and waking times.

By the American Academy of Sleep Medicine

Screen Time

Screen time management is important for teenagers as excessive use of electronic devices can affect sleep quality, concentration, eyesight and mental well-being. Spending too much time on screens can also reduce physical activity and interfere with academic performance. According to the American Academy of Pediatrics, balancing recreational screen use with other activities is important for healthy development.

  1. Limit recreational screen time to 2 hours per day.
  2. Take a 20-second break after every 20 minutes of screen use.
  3. Keep electronic devices away during meals.
  4. Stop screen use at least 1 hour before bedtime.
  5. Maintain at least 1 hour of physical activity daily.

By the American Academy of Pediatrics and American Optometric Association

Nutrition

Nutrition is essential for teenagers as their bodies undergo rapid growth and development. A balanced diet provides the energy and nutrients needed for healthy bones, muscle development, brain function and a strong immune system. Good nutrition also supports concentration, learning and overall physical and mental well-being. According to the World Health Organization, healthy eating habits during adolescence are important for long-term health.

  1. Consume at least 5 portions of fruits and vegetables daily.
  2. Aim for 3 balanced meals every day.
  3. Drink approximately 6 to 8 glasses of water daily.
  4. Include protein-rich foods in daily meals.
  5. Limit foods high in added sugar, salt and saturated fats.

By the World Health Organization
